Machine-learned potential energy surfaces for hot Criegee intermediates

Molecular dynamics, multireference corrections, and infrared spectra of CH₂OO and MVKO
Presented by
Jonathan M. SmithTemple University
Systems
CH₂OO · MVKO5 and 12 atoms; 4 MVKO conformers
Abstract

Criegee intermediates are short-lived carbonyl oxides formed when ozone attacks alkenes. They are born hot, they come in several conformers, and their electronic structure has partial multireference character, which makes their spectra and dynamics hard to compute directly. We train machine-learned potential energy surfaces on PSI4 density-functional data, add CASSCF corrections learned in normal-mode coordinates, and run molecular dynamics fast enough to sample hot molecules. For methyl vinyl ketone oxide (MVKO), we show why the standard dipole-autocorrelation route to the IR spectrum fails, replace it with harmonic dipole derivatives sampled along trajectories, and compare the result with experiment.

Significance

Why Criegee intermediates matter

Ozone + alkene → Criegee intermediate → atmospheric oxidation chemistry.

  • Carbonyl oxides (R₂COO) are formed in the ozonolysis of alkenes, including biogenic alkenes released by vegetation.
  • They are reactive oxidants: they decompose to OH radicals and react with SO₂ and water, which links them to sulfate aerosol and oxidant budgets.
  • MVKO, methyl vinyl ketone oxide, is a four-carbon Criegee intermediate formed from isoprene ozonolysis, so it sits on one of the largest atmospheric oxidation pathways.

What this project delivers

A tested computational pipeline that produces potential energy surfaces, dynamics and IR spectra for these molecules at a cost low enough to sample hot, conformationally mixed populations.

Why spectra

IR spectra are how these transient species are detected and identified in the lab. Reliable predicted spectra help assign bands and conformers in experiments such as Chung & Lee (2021).

Significance

Four things make these molecules hard to simulate

Born hot

Nascent Criegee intermediates from ozonolysis carry roughly 50–80 kcal/mol of internal energy (Stephenson & Lester 2020). For MVKO we model ≈ 50 kcal/mol, an effective temperature near 840 K.

Several conformers

MVKO has four low-lying conformers (syn/anti × cis/trans) within about 3 kcal/mol, each with its own spectrum.

Multireference character

The COO group has partial diradical character. Single-reference DFT can be off by 9–21 kcal/mol at distorted geometries; CASSCF/NEVPT2 is needed there.

Expensive reference data

Direct ab initio MD at this level is far too slow for the picosecond trajectories and thousands of snapshots a spectrum needs. A learned surface makes it affordable.

The PSI4 version crisis

December 2025 training data sat ≈ 8.5 kcal/mol below energies from the new PSI4 1.10 install. Mixing the two produced 15–40 kcal/mol systematic errors in validation. All old data was discarded and regenerated.

Rule since March 2026

Never mix training data from different PSI4 installations. Each training set records its PSI4 version and level of theory.

Dipole bug found on the way

A titled oeprop call stored dipoles under a different variable name, so every MVKO dipole read as zero until fixed.

Methods · learned surface

Coulomb-matrix descriptor with kernel ridge regression

Mij = ZiZj / rij  (i ≠ j),   Mii = ½ Zi2.4
  • Upper triangle as features: 15 for CH₂OO, 78 for MVKO. Atom order is fixed because the matrix is not permutation-invariant.
  • RBF-kernel KRR. StandardScaler on features and targets is essential: without it RMSE rises from ≈ 0.04 to ≈ 170 kcal/mol.
  • Kernel width γ scales inversely with descriptor size: 0.01 for CH₂OO, 0.001 for MVKO.
  • Force training is off; forces come from the energy surface.
Methods · training data

Training sets combine normal-mode grids and hot PSI4 dynamics

Normal-mode displacements fill the region near equilibrium and reach several hundred kcal/mol along stiff modes; PSI4 MD at 300, 600 and 1000 K samples the anharmonic, coupled motion. CH₂OO uses the same recipe: 344 frames (72 normal-mode, 202 MD, 70 validation).

Methods · forces and Hessian

Exact KRR forces and Hessians make dynamics and normal modes cheap

Because the KRR prediction is a sum of Gaussians in scaled descriptor space, its derivatives with respect to Cartesian coordinates follow by the chain rule through the descriptor Jacobian J.

F = 2γ σy JscT g
H = σy(−2γ)[ g·J2,sc + JT(−2γ Hdesc + Esc I) J ]

Implemented in modules/bakken.py (MLPESDriver); kernel weights and scaler statistics are cached at load time.

Validation · a lesson learned

More high-energy data made the surface worse near equilibrium

An adaptive loop added uncertainty-selected frames from 1000–2000 K PSI4 MD. Training RMSE improved to 0.203 kcal/mol over 1,300 frames, yet the model was unusable for spectra:

  • three imaginary modes at the PSI4 minimum (−1285, −880, −718 cm⁻¹);
  • pre-minimization landed on a saddle point, and ZPE initialization then pumped those modes to ≈ 1,619 K;
  • a later C–H stretch retrain (952 frames) lost the 893 cm⁻¹ O–O band and gained imaginary modes the same way.

Cause

Kernel weight is a shared resource. High-energy frames shift the global KRR fit away from the minimum and move the ML minimum off the true one.

Rules adopted

Always include the PSI4-optimized geometry as an anchor row. Use the near-equilibrium model (904 frames) for spectra. Handle H-atom wandering at the MD level with a bond guard, not with more high-energy data.

A low training RMSE is not enough; every surface now also passes a normal-mode check (next slide).

Validation · normal modes

Frequency test: kernel models are too stiff, MACE is not

Every kernel surface tested (Coulomb-matrix KRR, KRR in normal-mode coordinates, sGDML) put 5–12 of MVKO's 30 modes above 5000 cm⁻¹, up to 38,342 cm⁻¹ for sGDML. For the 1/r-based models the research log traces this to the curvature of 1/r features under RBF or Matérn kernels; hyperparameter tuning did not remove it.

The production MACE model learns local atomic energies with force-weighted training and has no modes above 5000 cm⁻¹. It still has one imaginary mode at its training-set equilibrium, so frequencies are validated per conformer before use. Reports: outputs/validate_anti_cis_nm_pes, outputs/validate_pes_20260418_101918.

Methods · multireference correction

Learning the CASSCF correction in normal-mode coordinates

q = UvibT M½ (R − Rref)
  • q = 0 at the equilibrium of each conformer, and ‖q‖² grows steadily with distortion energy.
  • Corrections δS₀ = ECASSCF − EDFT and the S₁, T₁ gaps are learned by KRR on q.
  • Grids: 4 amplitudes (0.5–2.0) along every mode, 232–240 points per conformer.
  • Applied as E = EML(R) + δ(q(R)) through the same driver interface, so it composes with KRR or MACE.

Why not Coulomb space?

At 300 K every MVKO geometry has kernel similarity K ≈ 0.999 in Coulomb space because heavy-atom charges dominate. Large corrections from distorted frames bled into equilibrium. In q-space, distorted frames are far away by construction.

Practical notes

MO coefficients cannot be reused across conformers (seeding anti-cis orbitals into syn-trans gave −3258 Ha). Frames with root switching or duplicate geometries are filtered by δS₀ and T₁-gap thresholds.

Results · multireference correction

All four conformer grids are complete and accurate

Leave-one-out errors are 0.03–0.65 kcal/mol for every state and conformer, well below kBT at 300 K (0.6 kcal/mol) for all but anti-cis δS₀. Anti conformers have higher S₁/T₁ gaps (25–29 kcal/mol) than syn (19–23). Data: outputs/casscf_wB97X_nm_grid_*/summary.json.

Interpretation

At 300 K the correction is within noise and does not change IR frequencies. For ozonolysis-energized MVKO (≈ 50 kcal/mol, Teff ≈ 840 K), the molecule reaches geometries where NEVPT2 lowers the energy by 9–21 kcal/mol relative to B3LYP. That should red-shift the O–O and C–O stretches.

Status

Marginal accuracy; kept for hot-molecule predictions only. A larger, cleaner NEVPT2 grid is a candidate next step.

Part 3

Infrared spectra of MVKO

Why the textbook molecular-dynamics route fails for this molecule, what replaces it, and how the result compares with experiment.

Spectroscopy · root cause

For MVKO, the dipole autocorrelation sees only torsions

Every ACF run for MVKO gave spectra dominated by ~150 cm⁻¹ features with no fingerprint signal. It persisted across Coulomb+KRR, retrained and MACE surfaces, B3LYP and ωB97X-D dipoles, thermostat settings, and 5 × 30,000-step trajectories.

Itors ∝ |μ₀|² θmax²  ≫  Istr ∝ (∂|μ|/∂qstr)²

The 4.5 D permanent dipole of the COO zwitterion rotates with every torsion. That term outweighs stretching contributions about 70,000-fold. It is a property of the molecule, not a bug, and no setting fixes it.

Spectroscopy · the fix

Harmonic dipole derivatives recover the fingerprint region

Ik ∝ ‖∂μ/∂qk‖², from the analytic gradient of the normal-mode KRR dipole surface

No MD is needed for the equilibrium spectrum. The strongest single mode is the 1088.8 cm⁻¹ C–O stretch; the 1011 and 1016 cm⁻¹ modes overlap into the tallest broadened band. Torsional intensity drops to 0.14 of maximum.

Spectroscopy · hot molecules

Local harmonic spectra along MACE trajectories

At each MD snapshot the local Hessian and dipole derivatives give a stick spectrum; averaging over snapshots adds thermal broadening and shifts. At 2000 K, anti-cis intensity moves from 1100–1170 cm⁻¹ toward ≈ 1025 and ≈ 1295 cm⁻¹, and syn-trans bands broaden and merge. Data: outputs/hot_emission_*/ir_hot_emission.csv.

Spectroscopy · experiment

Comparison with step-scan FTIR of MVKO

Experiment: 193 nm photolysis of an iodo precursor in 40 Torr Ar, 5–10 µs gate, 0.5 cm⁻¹ resolution; seven bands B₁–B₇ between 930 and 1460 cm⁻¹ (digitized from Chung & Lee 2021, Fig. 3a).

  • The 300 K anti-cis spectrum places strong bands near B₅ and B₄ and a pair near B₂/B₁, offset by roughly 5–20 cm⁻¹; B₇ and B₆ appear only weakly.
  • The 2000 K spectrum does not match this time gate, which suggests the molecules have largely relaxed in 40 Torr Ar by 5–10 µs.
  • Next: Boltzmann-weighted sum over all four conformers and a time-resolved comparison.

An early force-unit slip left accelerations ~10²⁰ too small: MD that "ran" but never moved.

  • Scale features and targets (StandardScaler) for every KRR model.
  • Never mix PSI4 installations in one training set.
  • Anchor every retrain with the optimized geometry.
  • Validate frequencies, not just RMSE, before using a surface for spectra.
  • A flat spectrum means check the motion first, then the FFT.
  • Equilibrate after heating; cap parallel jobs at 2 on Apple Silicon to avoid kernel watchdog panics.
  • Define model classes at module scope so they pickle.

Roadmap

Next steps

Spectra that match the experiment's conditions

Boltzmann-weighted spectra over all four conformers with CASSCF-corrected MACE surfaces; time-resolved comparison as molecules cool.

Multi-state dynamics

Extend pes_family.py from blending conformer surfaces to S₀/S₁/T₁ families with mixing or hopping, using the learned gaps.

MACE quality

Remove the remaining imaginary mode with per-conformer anchors and validate each conformer against its PSI4 Hessian.

More molecules

Apply the pipeline to other substituted Criegee intermediates, such as methacrolein oxide and 2-butenal oxide.

Reproducibility

Move the default branch back to the complete main, tag result-producing commits, and extend unit tests to bakken.py, nm_pes.py and mace_pes.py.

Collaboration

Shared, versioned surfaces and spectra that experimental groups can use for band and conformer assignment.
