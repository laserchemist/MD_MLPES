J. M. Smith · Temple University · 6 · K. T. Kuwata, J. Phys. Chem. A 128, 7331–7345 (2024), doi 10.1021/acs.jpca.4c04517

Kuwata, "Computational Modeling of the Conformation-Dependent Atmospheric Reactivity of Criegee Intermediates," J. Phys. Chem. A 2024, 128, 7331-7345 (Perspective). Points used here: (1) syn-alkyl and syn-vinyl CIs isomerize quickly to vinyl hydroperoxides or dioxoles; CH2OO and anti CIs prefer dioxirane closure or bimolecular reaction (water, SO2). (2) Single-reference methods with conventional TST and Eckart tunneling have matched experiment for ground-state reactivity. (3) For the CH2OO B-X spectrum, MRCI+Q-F12b(18,14) gives vertical excitation 3.75-3.83 eV, EOM-CCSD 4.01 eV (too high: no static correlation); CASPT2(12,10) beat (10,8) and MRCI(10,8) (McCoy et al.), so a larger active space matters more than the level of dynamic correlation; TDDFT is about 0.7 eV too blue. For MVK-oxide smaller active spaces, including (8,6), shifted lambda-max 50-100 nm red. (4) Nikoobakht and Koppel trace the vibrational structure to the B/C avoided crossing near R(O-O) 2.1 A, using R(O-O), theta(COO) and R(C-O): the coordinates of our 3-D surface. (5) Roaming of OH in vinyl hydroperoxide decay needs multireference electronic structure and variational TST. Our related precedent: Smith, Nikow, Ma, Wilhelm, Han, Sharma, Bowman and Dai, 'Chemical Activation through Super Energy Transfer Collisions,' J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2014, 136, 1682-1685: hot H atoms (about 60 kcal/mol) form a transient complex with HCCH, and about 10% of collisions leave up to 70% of the energy in acetylene vibration, seen by time-resolved IR emission and reproduced by quasi-classical trajectories on a global ab initio potential (Bowman group). Long-lived complexes on a flat region of the surface are the same dynamical regime as roaming, and the same IR-emission and trajectory tools apply. Also noted: Nguyen et al. attribute almost all OH from ethene ozonolysis to primary-ozonide diradicals, not to CH2OO.
