Sharp Q branches are quantum line stacking: classical dynamics could not give them.

J. M. Smith · Temple University · 27 · cm⁻¹; expt.: Su et al., Science 340, 174 (2013); ν₄ ratios: Chang et al., JCP 146, 244302 (2017)

Each band is built from the VCI origin and intensity, the a/b/c character of its transition moment, and rotational constants for the upper and lower level (computed changes from the ground state, ground state from experiment). 300 K, 1 cm-1 line width. The O-O band origin was shifted by -0.8 cm-1 onto the experimental origin for the overlay (bottom left); nothing else is adjusted. With rigid constants (every level the same as the ground state) the shape correlation drops from 0.91 to 0.76, so the vibrational dependence of A, B, C matters for the contour. The hot emission at 13 kJ/mol (bottom right) adds hot bands on the red side; the experimental hot band nu6 + nu7 - nu7 is at 899.8 cm-1 (Huang et al. 2015), where our emission shows a peak at 900. Earlier in the deck the contour came from classical trajectories (each a P/R doublet at plus or minus the rotation frequency); this is the quantum version of the same physics, now with the Q branch.
