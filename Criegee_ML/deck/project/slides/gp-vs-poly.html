A Gaussian process says where it is unsure; a polynomial does not

J. M. Smith · Temple University · 38 · GP: diabml.gpdelta, Matérn 5/2, fitted length scale 0.18 Å

Same 11 Delta points as the Delta figure. A polynomial is one global formula: it fits the points well but carries its trend past the data, here diving to -400 meV by 1.7 A, and gives no warning. A GP builds the curve from the data with a kernel that sets the smoothness; with a zero prior mean it relaxes back to no correction over about one length scale (0.18 A here) and its error band widens as it does. That error band comes from the same fit: it drives the choice of the next shell batches (largest sigma) and, by drawing posterior samples, gives error bars on the fundamentals. Limits: sigma is interpolation error only, not the error of CCSD(T); it must be checked on held-out batches (shell_check does). The GP's own relaxation to zero is a soft switch, but its length scale is learned from the well, so the explicit switch w stays. Use: polynomial for the dense, smooth well data in vib_levels; GP for Delta on the network across the sparse shell.
