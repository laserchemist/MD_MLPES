VCI, vibrational configuration interaction: mixing simple vibrations

1

Simple states first. Treat each of the 9 normal modes as its own spring; a state is how many quanta each one has, e.g. one quantum of C–O stretch.

2

The real surface couples them. States of similar energy mix, like electron configurations in CI.

3

Matrix and diagonalise: 36,890 simple states; each level is named by its largest part.

Why it matters: 2ν₉ has no intensity of its own, but by mixing with ν₄ it borrows some and appears in the spectrum, at 1229 cm⁻¹ (expt. 1234).

J. M. Smith · Temple University · 24 · levels from this work; a grid like DVR in 9-D would need ~10¹² points

For an undergraduate audience. A grid (DVR) works well for 3 or 4 coordinates but its size grows as points to the power of the number of coordinates: 20 points in 9 coordinates is 5 x 10^11 grid points. VCI instead starts from the harmonic picture students know (each normal mode a separate oscillator with levels n h nu) and builds the true states as combinations of a manageable number of these product states, chosen up to a cutoff energy (here 7000 cm-1 for the low modes, up to 3 quanta in the C-H stretches). The name comes from configuration interaction in electronic structure, where Slater determinants (electron configurations) are mixed the same way. The example on the slide is a Fermi resonance: the C-O stretch nu4 and two quanta of the CH2 torsion 2nu9 lie close in energy and the anharmonic surface couples them, so the true levels are mixtures that repel each other. Experiment sees both (Huang et al. 2015 assigned the 1234 band to 2nu9 for this reason). Numbers: harmonic nu4 1308.7, 2 x nu9 harmonic 1274.4; VCI 1275.1 (69% nu4, 21% 2nu9) and 1228.6 (65% 2nu9, 22% nu4).
