Spectra from dynamics on a learned surface

Surface = energy + Δ + dipole. Dynamics on it, then the Fourier transform of the dipole autocorrelation gives a spectrum to compare with experiment.

Since the poster, for CH₂OO:

a full 9-D CCSD(T) surface for the well, quantum vibrations (VCI) and ring-polymer dynamics to test it, and a quasi-diabatic NEVPT2 network with Δ-learning for the excited states and hot molecules.

J. M. Smith · Temple University · 7 · B. N. Simiyu and J. M. Smith, poster, ACS Fall 2025 National Meeting

Figure 1 of the poster: Simiyu and Smith, "Vibrational spectral prediction of transient species using machine learning potential energy surfaces," ACS Fall 2025 (Temple University; Hylleraas Centre, University of Oslo). There the surfaces were trained on importance-sampled points from DFT (B3LYP/cc-pVTZ) direct dynamics, with CCSD(T) and a Delta correction; the example panels show a small test molecule (O-H stretch and torsion). The rest of this deck is the sequence of refinements that took CH2OO from that protocol to agreement with experiment, and why each was needed.
