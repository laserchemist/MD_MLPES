Ring-polymer molecular dynamics · the idea

RPMD: each quantum atom a necklace of beads

The trick (Feynman)

In its statistics each quantum atom is a classical ring of P beads on springs (ωP = P kBT/ħ); plain MD of the rings then gives zero-point energy and the spread of the atoms.

Exact for a harmonic bond

Approximate for anharmonic ones: here it closes 25–78% of the gap to VCI.

The springs’ own vibrations

The lowest ring mode is ≈ 2πkBT/hc = 1310 cm⁻¹ at 300 K for any P: on the C–O band. TRPMD damps it with a thermostat, which broadens bands.

Still classical in time

No quantum mixing (Fermi resonances, overtones), no sharp Q branch: those need VCI.

J. M. Smith · Temple University · 30 · Craig & Manolopoulos, JCP 121, 3368 (2004); TRPMD: Rossi, Ceriotti & Manolopoulos, JCP 140, 234116 (2014)

For an undergraduate audience. Feynman's path integral says the quantum Boltzmann distribution of a particle is the same as the classical distribution of a closed chain (ring polymer) of P copies, the beads, each feeling 1/P of the potential and joined to its neighbours by harmonic springs of frequency omega_P = P kB T / hbar; the result becomes exact as P grows (the planning stage rpmd_plan shows 97% of the zero-point energy captured at P = 32 for the C-H stretches, 99.8% at P = 128). The ring's size shows the quantum spread of the atom: about 0.16 A for H and a few hundredths of an A for C and O at 300 K. RPMD (Craig and Manolopoulos 2004) goes one step further and runs Newtonian dynamics of the rings, using the bead-averaged dipole to compute the spectrum. Why harmonic springs matter twice. First, for a harmonic potential the ring's centre moves exactly like the quantum oscillator (the Kubo-transformed correlation function is exact), so RPMD is exact in the harmonic limit and errors come only from anharmonicity. Second, the springs between beads are themselves vibrations, with frequencies omega_k = 2 omega_P sin(k pi / P); the lowest tends to 2 pi kB T / hbar, about 1310 cm-1 at 300 K, whatever P is (1308, 2603, 3874 cm-1 for P = 32, drawn on the right). These unphysical vibrations can resonate with real ones, here the C-O stretch, and put spurious peaks or splittings in the spectrum. Thermostatted RPMD (TRPMD, Rossi, Ceriotti and Manolopoulos 2014) attaches a friction to the ring's internal modes only (gamma_k = 2 lambda omega_k, lambda 0.5 here), which removes the resonances but broadens the bands; our friction scan from 0 to 1 did not move the band centres. What remains classical: the motion of the ring's centre, so quantum interference between states (Fermi resonances such as nu4 / 2nu9, overtone and combination bands) and quantized rotation (the Q branch) are missing. That is why TRPMD sits 5-6 cm-1 above VCI on the same surface, and why VCI is the reference wherever it can be done. For MVK-oxide, where VCI cannot cover all 30 modes, TRPMD calibrated this way is the practical quantum method.
