J. M. Smith · Temple University · 31 · 9-D CCSD(T) surface and CCSD(T) dipole; classical (P = 1) and TRPMD (P = 16) trajectories

Two real trajectories on the 9-D CCSD(T) surface with the CCSD(T) dipole surface, run with the same code as the rpmd9d stage (ring-polymer integrator, PILE thermostat during 1 ps of equilibration at 300 K, then 1 ps shown at 0.25 fs time steps, one frame every 2 fs). Left: one bead per atom, which is ordinary classical molecular dynamics. Right: 16 beads per atom with the TRPMD thermostat on the ring's internal modes; the translucent spheres are the centroids (bead averages) joined by the bonds, the small spheres are the beads joined in rings. Measured spreads (root-mean-square distance of the beads from their centroid): H 0.15-0.16 A, C 0.05 A, O 0.05 A. The blue arrow is the dipole moment from the centre of mass (bead-averaged on the right), drawn about 0.3 A per debye; CH2OO has a dipole of about 4 D. Things to point out to students: the molecule tumbles slowly (a full turn takes a few picoseconds at 300 K) while the bonds vibrate many times faster; the infrared spectrum comes from how the dipole changes, both from the vibrations (band positions) and the turning (band shape). The molecule's overall drift through space has been removed. The animation is also available as an MP4 for talks.
