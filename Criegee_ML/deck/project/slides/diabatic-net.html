What the well surface cannot do: dissociation, the UV states, couplings for surface hopping

The 9-D CCSD(T) surface joins it as a Δ on the diagonal: spectroscopic accuracy in the well, multistate physics beyond

J. M. Smith · Temple University · 35 · learned diabats (dashed) behind CASSCF states along r(O–O); see the “Bridging the Middle Zone” deck

This is the larger model the CH2OO well surface plugs into, and the one we build on for multistate dynamics. Instead of fitting each adiabatic state separately (they have kinks and avoided crossings that a network fits badly), the network predicts a small symmetric matrix H(R) whose diagonal elements are diabatic states with fixed chemical character and whose off-diagonal elements are couplings; diagonalising it gives the adiabatic energies that the electronic-structure calculation produces, and the eigenvectors give the character of each state and the nonadiabatic couplings needed for surface hopping. For CH2OO the diabats are physically framed: the ion pair CH2O+ + O- (the zwitterionic character that makes the Criegee intermediate a bound molecule), CH2O(T1) + O(3P), and the three components of CH2O + O(1D). They cross near r(O-O) 2-2.5 A, which is where the B/C avoided crossing and the UV structure come from (Kuwata 2024) and where hot or photoexcited molecules break the O-O bond. Complementary to the 9-D CCSD(T) surface: the network is trained on multireference (CASSCF/NEVPT2) data and covers every state everywhere at modest accuracy; the CCSD(T) surface is spectroscopically accurate but only for the ground state in the well. The next three slides show how they are joined: Delta = CCSD(T) minus the network's ground state, added to every diagonal element (which leaves gaps and couplings unchanged) and switched off where the states crowd together. The same construction is what we would build for MVK-oxide, whose conformers and resonance-stabilised C=C-C=O-O framework add more diabats. Background on the idea: the "Bridging the Middle Zone" deck.
