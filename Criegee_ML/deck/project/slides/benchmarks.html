J. M. Smith · Temple University · 5 · energy increases down the table

IR absorption: Su, Huang, Witek and Lee, Science 2013, 340, 174, step-scan FTIR of CH2OO from CH2I + O2; bands at 1435, 1286, 1241, 908 and 848 cm-1. IR emission: time-resolved FTIR emission from highly vibrationally excited molecules, as in Smith, Nikow, Ma, Wilhelm, Han, Sharma, Bowman and Dai, JACS 2014, 136, 1682 (super energy transfer collisions) and Smith, Nikow, Wilhelm and Dai, J. Phys. Chem. A 2023, 127, 8782 (hot acetylene relaxation via vinylidene). Emission from hot CH2OO itself is a target: we have not found a published spectrum. First study: CH2I2 photolysis in the presence of O2 (CH2I + O2 gives CH2OO + I) with CH2OO carrying 13 kJ/mol above its zero point; the ir stage's 13 kJ/mol microcanonical runs are the computed counterpart. One surface has to pass all three probes, because ozonolysis-energy dynamics crosses all three regions. UV photolysis: Beames, Liu, Lu and Lester, JACS 2012, 134, 20045 (UV spectrum, prompt dissociation near 335 nm); Samanta, Beames, Lester and Subotnik, J. Chem. Phys. 2014, 141, doi 10.1063/1.4894746 (quantum dynamics on coupled singlet states); Esposito et al., J. Phys. Chem. A 2021, 125, doi 10.1021/acs.jpca.1c03643 (velocity map imaging and trajectories: O(1D) + H2CO and O(3P) + H2CO channels through conical intersections). Methods for Criegee UV spectra are reviewed by Kuwata, J. Phys. Chem. A 2024, 128, 7331 (next slide).
