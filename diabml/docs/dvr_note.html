Quantum vibrational levels of CH₂OO on the 3-D surface

diabml note, 1 November 2026 · stages gs_grid and vib_levels · runs 24_spec, 26_gs

Result. On a smooth state-specific CASSCF(8,6)+SC-NEVPT2/cc-pVDZ ground-state grid, the quantum fundamentals of the 3-D model (r(O–O), ∠COO, r(C–O); CH₂ rigid) are COO 563, O–O 937, C–O 1365 cm⁻¹, stable to ±3–6 cm⁻¹ over ten fits. O–O is 29 cm⁻¹ above experiment (908). The earlier spread of 915–1161 cm⁻¹ in the harmonic O–O frequency came from the SA4 reference data, not from the method.

Why a quantum calculation

The IR spectra from dynamics (power spectrum of dμ/dt) give band shapes: rotational contours and the anharmonicity and broadening of a hot molecule. At 300 K or 13 kJ/mol a classical trajectory stays near the bottom of the well, so its band positions are close to the harmonic frequencies. The real molecule always carries zero-point energy, which puts it further out on the anharmonic part of the potential. The vibrational levels on the same surface give the band positions including that effect, and are compared directly with experiment and with the VPT2 values of Su, Huang, Witek & Lee, Science 340, 174 (2013).

Method

Hamiltonian. J = 0, in the model's internal coordinates q = (r(O–O), ∠COO, r(C–O)), the CH₂ group rigid (r(C–H) 1.08 Å, ∠HCO 120°) and moving with C: H = −(ħ²/2) Σij ∂i Gij(q) ∂j + V(q). G is the vibrational block of the inverse of the full metric JᵀMJ, built from the Cartesian derivatives of the atom positions with respect to the three internal coordinates, the three translations and the three infinitesimal rotations, so overall translation and rotation are removed exactly at J = 0. The small extrapotential of the exact (Podolsky) form is left out; it is nearly constant over the well.

Discretisation. Sinc discrete variable representation (DVR; Colbert & Miller, J. Chem. Phys. 96, 1982 (1992)): a uniform grid per coordinate (28 points; 28³ ≈ 22 000 in all) spanning the harmonic classically allowed region up to 0.6–1.2 eV. In a DVR the potential is diagonal (its value at each grid point); the position-dependent kinetic operator is applied as Σ Diᵀ Gij Dj with the sinc first-derivative matrices D. The lowest 16 states come from an iterative eigensolver without storing H (about 5 s).

Assignment and intensities. Each state is labelled by its largest overlap with the states of the harmonic problem on the same grid (quadratic V, G at the minimum), whose quantum numbers are known; modes are named by their potential energy distribution. Intensities are ν·|⟨0|μ|v⟩|² with the body-frame dipole of the charge-model dipole surface.

Checks. On a quadratic potential the grid reproduces the analytic G–F harmonic frequencies to 0.01 cm⁻¹; the density of each state on the outer grid layers is below 10⁻³ for the fundamentals.

The data: why the surface had to be recomputed

The first run used the polynomial well fitted to the 24_spec reference data, SA4-CASSCF(8,6)+SC-NEVPT2. The quantum levels themselves were well converged. The curvature of the polynomial was not: refitting with polynomial orders 2–4 and energy windows of 0.3–0.9 eV moved the harmonic O–O frequency over 915–1161 cm⁻¹, ten times the anharmonic shift being measured. Even after the track stage, a quartic fit to the tracked points left 6.5 meV of scatter. The cause is the top root of the four-state average (9–11 eV up): its energy jumps by up to 2.5 eV between neighbouring points, the averaged orbitals change with it, and S0 moves by 5–50 meV. Tracking compares only S1–S2, so it cannot see this.

The gs_grid stage (run 26_gs) therefore computed a dedicated ground-state grid with ORCA 6.1.1, 6 parallel jobs, 27 min:

  • 11 × 11 × 11 = 1331 points around the 24_spec minimum: r(O–O) 1.202–1.502 Å (step 0.03), ∠COO 103.1–133.1° (step 3°), r(C–O) 1.152–1.402 Å (step 0.025);
  • state-specific CASSCF(8,6)/cc-pVDZ + SC-NEVPT2, no state averaging;
  • every point started from the converged orbitals of its neighbours along a spanning tree from the centre (two starts per point). In 1133 comparisons the two starts never differed by more than 4×10⁻⁵ mEh: a single solution everywhere.

What the comparison says

  • Band positions are now limited by the electronic structure and the model, not the fit. O–O 937 vs 908 (+29) and the harmonic 944–958 vs NEVPT2/aVDZ 916. Likely contributions: the basis (cc-pVDZ here, diffuse aug-cc-pVDZ in Su et al.), the small active space, and the CH₂ frame held at r(C–H) 1.08 Å, ∠HCO 120° instead of relaxed.
  • The zero-point anharmonic shift of O–O is smaller than VPT2's: −7 to −15 cm⁻¹ across fits, against −24. Part of the VPT2 shift comes from coupling to CH₂ motion, which a rigid CH₂ excludes.
  • C–O cannot be compared mode for mode. In the molecule it mixes with the CH₂ scissor and its intensity splits between 1286 and 1435 cm⁻¹ (42 + 33 = 75 relative to O–O). The 3-D model puts it all in one band, at intensity 0.96. This needs the CH₂ coordinates.
  • Intensity ordering is right: O–O strongest, C–O close to it, COO bend weak.

How this fits with the dynamics

Classical dynamics on the same surface gives the band shapes (P/Q/R rotational contour, hot-band broadening), and the DVR gives the zero-point shift classical dynamics lacks. The IR stage now uses both. With [ir] well_source = "gs_grid" it runs on this surface. Its contour figure (IR4) shows the O–O band as computed and moved by the DVR shift (fundamental − harmonic), so only the shape is compared with experiment. The zoom figure (IR3) marks the DVR fundamentals.
