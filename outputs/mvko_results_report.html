MVKO Multi-State ML-PES: Hot-Molecule IR Emission Results

Methyl vinyl ketone oxide (MVKO, C₄H₆O₂) — anti-cis conformer. Anti-cis = Criegee-O pointing anti to the C=C, methyl cis to the terminal oxide. Date: 2026-06-06.

Key Findings

  • ACF-based IR fails for MVKO: the 4.5 D permanent COO dipole makes torsional reorientation dominate the autocorrelation function by ~70,000× over vibrational stretching. No thermostat tuning, ZPE settings, or improved dipole surface can fix this.
  • Correct approach: harmonic dipole derivatives from NMDipoleSurface.ir_intensities() + local-harmonic hot emission (trajectory-averaged instantaneous frequencies and intensities).
  • Strongest IR peak: mode 15 at 1089 cm⁻¹ (C-O stretch) at 300 K; shifts to 1144 cm⁻¹ at 2000 K with anharmonic redshift.
  • Temperature effect: at 2000 K, modes 22-24 show >300 cm⁻¹ broadening (torsion/COO bend coupling); C-H region (modes 25-30) shifts upward by 100-400 cm⁻¹ due to anharmonic stiffening at high amplitude.
  • S1 gap: 29.0 kcal/mol at equilibrium; T1 gap: 26.5 kcal/mol. Both gaps increase slightly along the O-O stretch coordinate.
  • MACE PES validation: 0 imaginary modes, 0 unphysical modes (>5000 cm⁻¹), max C-H ~3200 cm⁻¹ — confirms physical Hessian curvature.

Section 1: Method Overview

Molecule

MVKO (methyl vinyl ketone oxide): (CH₂=CH)(CH₃)C(O⁻O⁺), 12 atoms, 30 vibrational modes. Criegee intermediate from ozonolysis of methyl vinyl ketone (MVK). Atmospherically relevant reactive intermediate. Atom ordering: C1(Criegee), O1(proximal), O2(distal), C2(vinyl=CH-), C3(=CH₂), C4(methyl), H1-H6. Anti-cis conformer: equilibrium O-O bond length 1.3446 Å.

Two-Layer ML Architecture

The potential energy surface uses a two-layer correction on the wB97X-D/6-31G* baseline:

  • ES0(R) = EwB97X-ML(R) + δS0-ML(R) — ground state, Coulomb+KRR base + CASSCF(4,4) multi-reference correction
  • ES1(R) = ES0(R) + ΔgapS1-ML(R) — first excited singlet
  • ET1(R) = ES0(R) + ΔgapT1-ML(R) — lowest triplet (ISC)

CASSCF active space: (4,4) with frontier COO biradical orbitals {n⁺(Oterm), n⁻(Oterm), π(COO), π*(COO)}. Natural orbital occupations at eq: (1.948, 1.724, 0.275, 0.052) — 14% biradical character. Delta-ML models use normal-mode-coordinate KRR (RBF kernel) for δS0, ΔgapS1, ΔgapT1.

Dipole Surface

NMDipoleSurface: KRR in normal-mode coordinates with analytic ∂μ/∂qk. R² = 0.9997 on wB97X-D/6-31G* training dipoles (250 frames, 2 rounds GP active learning). C-H stretch modes (25-30) have non-zero ∂μ/∂qCH by design — absent in Coulomb+KRR descriptor.

Why ACF IR Fails for MVKO

The dipole autocorrelation function (DACF) approach assumes that the dominant signal is vibrational. For MVKO, the permanent COO dipole (μperm ≈ 4.5 D) is ~70,000× larger than the vibrational amplitude (δμ ≈ 0.05 D per vibrational period). Any torsional reorientation — even sub-degree — completely overwhelms the vibrational signal. This is not fixable by improved dipole surfaces, thermostats, or trajectory length.

Correct approach: harmonic dipole derivatives at each trajectory snapshot (local harmonic approximation), giving ir_hot_emission(T) = ⟨ Σk ||∂μ/∂qk||² δ(ν - νk,local) ⟩traj.

Section 4b: Theoretical Basis & Computational Justification

Why DACF-Based MD-IR Fails for MVKO

The standard MD infrared spectrum uses the dipole autocorrelation function (DACF):

I(ν) ∝ ν · Re ∫0∞ ⟨μ(0)·μ(t)⟩ e2πiνt dt

This is exact in the classical limit (Gordon 1965; Berens & Wilson 1981). However, MVKO carries a ~4.5 D permanent COO− dipole. Torsional fluctuations reorient it by angle θ, contributing ACF power ∝ |μ0|2θmax2ωtors2, while a stretching mode contributes only (∂|μ|/∂q)2⟨q2⟩. With |μ0| = 4.5 D and ∂|μ|/∂qC–O ≈ 0.13 D, torsional DACF power exceeds the C–O stretching contribution by ~70,000×. This problem is well known for polar condensed-phase systems (Gaigeot & Sprik 2003) and cannot be fixed by improving the PES or dipole model.

Step-by-Step: Trajectory-Averaged Local Harmonic Intensities

The adopted method uses the local harmonic approximation (LHA) grounded in instantaneous normal mode (INM) theory (Stratt 1995; Keyes 1997):

1
Generate thermal ensemble via MACE ML-MD. Five Velocity-Verlet + Berendsen trajectories on the MACE wB97X-D/6-31G* + CASSCF δS0 surface. ZPE-floor initialization populates each mode to at least its zero-point energy level.
2
Sample 500 decorrelated geometries at stride Δt = 150 fs, providing a Boltzmann-weighted ensemble ρ(R) ∝ exp[−E(R)/kBT].
3
Compute instantaneous Hessian via FD on MACE analytic forces (72 evaluations per geometry). Mass-weighting and diagonalization give INM frequencies ωk(Ri) and eigenvectors (Cho et al. 1994; Stratt & Cho 1994).
4
Evaluate local IR intensities via NMDipoleSurface: Ik(R) = ||∂μ/∂qk(R)||2. This KRR model (R2=0.9997) captures only internal dipole changes along each local mode — immune to permanent-dipole reorientation.
5
Accumulate Lorentzian peaks and average over all sampled geometries:

S(ν) = (1/N) ∑i ∑k Ik(Ri) · γ2 / [(ν − ωk(Ri))2 + γ2]

The distribution of local frequencies encodes anharmonic broadening and mode-coupling via the geometry-dependent Hessian, analogous to the quantum-corrected spectral approach of Ramírez et al. (2004) applied to local INM intensities rather than velocity autocorrelations.

Classical Validity — Quantum–Classical Crossover

Classical trajectory propagation is justified when ℏωk/kBT < 1. At 2000 K (kBT = 1390 cm−1), all modes below ~2000 cm−1 are effectively classical. Only C–H stretches (>3000 cm−1) remain significantly quantum mechanical. At 300 K most modes are quantum — the 300 K spectrum is best understood as a near-equilibrium harmonic approximation rather than a true anharmonic classical result.
